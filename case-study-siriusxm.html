Case Study 01

Driving customer behavior toward digital self-service.

Role

UX Director, Practice Founder/Lead

Scale

12M monthly visitors across SiriusXM digital properties, including 3M monthly account management users

Focus

Self-service, trial conversion, billing, subscriptions, online account management, content discovery

Methods

UX strategy, research, IA, prototyping, usability testing, A/B testing, design systems

At SiriusXM, I helped build and lead the in-house UX practice behind a broader shift toward digital self-service. Over time, digital transaction mix grew from 18% to 50%, contributing to millions in call-deflection savings and a lift in trial subscription conversion. That shift was not the result of one redesign. It came from a broader body of UX, product, research, testing, and optimization work.

This case study highlights two representative efforts — the Online Account Center and Channel Guide — that show how I helped turn customer behavior into clearer product direction.

Business Context

SiriusXM needed more customers to complete high-value actions online.

Millions of customers were already engaging with SiriusXM digitally, but many important actions still depended on support channels, fragmented flows, or product logic that customers had to learn.

The business needed clearer digital paths for the behaviors that mattered most: paying bills, converting from trial, managing subscriptions, adding radios, accessing streaming, and discovering content worth keeping.

For customers, this meant less confusion.

For the business, it meant call deflection, conversion, retention, operational efficiency, and stronger digital self-service.

Digital self-service objectives

01

Deflect calls.

Drive customers toward self-service paths for billing, payments, and account management.

02

Grow digital transactions.

Improve paths to trial conversion, upgrades, add-ons, inactive-radio reactivation, and account changes.

03

Strengthen product value.

Help customers discover the programming, channels, shows, and perks that make the service worth paying for after the trial is over.

04

Scale what worked.

Create reusable UX patterns, testing practices, and modular product structures that could improve more than one area over time.

How I Led the Work

I built and led the UX capability behind digital self-service growth.

To move more customer transactions online, SiriusXM needed more than redesigned interfaces. We needed a way to understand what customers were trying to do, measure where they struggled, test better paths, and keep optimizing the journeys that mattered most.

I led that effort from a UX perspective — building the practice, research approach, voice-of-customer inputs, A/B and user testing capability, measurement framework, and reusable design systems that helped teams move from opinion to evidence.

That engine powered work across billing, trial conversion, subscription management, upgrades, radio additions, streaming access, and content discovery. Through sustained UX, product, testing, and optimization work, digital transaction mix grew from 18% to 50% — helping reduce call-center demand and improve trial subscription conversion. The examples below show how that system worked in practice.

What We Learned

Payment and trial-conversion paths were clear. Subscription tasks exposed deeper friction around accounts, radios, devices, plans, and actions.

Users wanted search, struggled to find shows, relied on inconsistent site search, and needed clearer content labels and cues.

UX Strategy

Improve IA, labeling, dashboard hierarchy, accordion behavior, and task pathways across account and subscription management.

Prioritize search, improve genre sorting, clarify channel summaries, design for new visitors, and make the experience more modular.

Connecting the Dots

Better systems created better outcomes.

The Online Account Center showed that self-service friction was not just a screen-design problem. Customers needed clearer relationships between accounts, subscriptions, radios, devices, payments, and actions.

The Channel Guide showed that content discovery was not just a browsing problem. It was tied directly to perceived value, acquisition, retention, search behavior, and customer confidence.

The larger contribution was giving stakeholders a better way to make decisions: customer feedback, testing, measurement, optimization, reusable patterns, and UX strategy connected to business outcomes.

The shift happened through sustained, connected work: clearer paths, better evidence, stronger patterns, and continuous optimization across the journeys that mattered most. Together, that work helped move millions of high-value customer actions into digital self-service — growing digital transaction mix from 18% to 50% and turning UX capability into measurable business impact.
